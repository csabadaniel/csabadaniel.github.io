Top Attractions & Things to Do with Kids

Public transport is preferred. Milan city-centre attractions are walkable from the main train station and have no separate access note. All other attractions include a Getting there note.

For the Whole Family

  • Ferrari Museum (Maranello, near Modena/Bologna) — simulators, iconic cars, and a motorsport history that appeals to all ages; easy day trip from Bologna or Modena. Getting there: train to Modena (~25 min from Bologna), then SETA bus 780 to Maranello (~25 min); buses are infrequent — check timetable in advance.
  • Cinque Terre — colourful hilltop villages, easy coastal walks, gelato stops; Vernazza and Manarola are the most photogenic, best enjoyed on foot between villages or by boat. Getting there: Trenitalia regional train on the Genoa–La Spezia line; each village has its own station. Buy a Cinque Terre Card which bundles train hops and trail access.
  • Pisa: Leaning Tower & Piazza dei Miracoli — the tower, baptistery and cathedral together are genuinely stunning; fun for photos (the classic “holding it up” shot) and impressive for all ages. Getting there: 20 min walk from Pisa Centrale station, or take LAM Rossa bus (stops outside the station) for a 5 min ride.
  • Milan’s Duomo — interior & rooftop walk — the soaring Gothic interior is unmissable; the rooftop terraces are hands-on and give great views over the city
  • Pizza-making class — widely available in larger cities; interactive and memorable for all ages

Especially for the 13-year-old Boy

  • Leonardo da Vinci National Museum of Science & Technology (Milan) — interactive science exhibits, full-scale ship models, historical technology; one of the largest science museums in Europe
  • FC Internazionale / AC Milan stadium tour (San Siro, Milan) — stadium tour and museum; football-mad boys will love it. Getting there: Metro M5 (Lilla) to San Siro Stadio, or M1 (Rossa) to Lotto then ~20 min walk; not central but very easy by metro.
  • Monza Formula 1 Circuit — museum and track tours available; close to Milan (30 min), strong motorsport tie-in with the Ferrari Museum visit. Getting there: train from Milan Centrale to Monza (~15 min), then 25 min walk through the park or a short taxi ride to the circuit entrance.

Especially for the 10-year-old Girl

  • Castello Sforzesco (Milan) — a real castle with towers and a moat; castle exploration is reliably exciting at this age
  • Chocolate and gelato workshops — widely available across northern Italian cities; hands-on and she gets to eat the result

Parents’ Must-Sees

  • The Last Supper (Cenacolo Vinciano, Milan) — Leonardo da Vinci’s mural; tickets must be booked months in advance, strictly timed entry; non-negotiable if passing through Milan
  • Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II (Milan) — one of the world’s oldest and most beautiful shopping arcades; worth a stroll and an overpriced coffee
  • Portofino & Santa Margherita Ligure — charming Ligurian harbour towns; Portofino especially is a classic adult bucket-list stop. Getting there: train from Genoa to Santa Margherita Ligure-Portofino station (~35 min), then local bus 782 (~25 min) or a seasonal ferry to Portofino harbour.
  • Verona’s Arena & old town — a near-perfectly preserved Roman amphitheatre; the medieval streets and Juliet’s balcony make for an excellent half-day. Getting there: Verona Porta Nuova station is served by fast trains from Milan (~1 h 10 min); the Arena and old town are a 20 min walk or short bus ride (lines 11/12/13) from the station.

Seasonal Notes (Late October)

  • Halloween season means some attractions run themed events — check dates carefully
  • Autumn colours are beautiful across northern Italy and the Tuscan hills
  • Crowds are low compared to summer; many outdoor sites are pleasant to visit
  • Some beach-side Cinque Terre paths and boat services may have reduced hours — verify before visiting
